Hypertension (high blood pressure) is a major cause of premature death worldwide. The WHO estimates that 1.28 billion adults aged 30-79 years have hypertension, with two-thirds living in low- and middle-income countries. Hypertension is defined as systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg. Most people with hypertension have no symptoms, which is why it is called the "silent killer." Complications include heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and dementia. Prevention includes reducing salt intake (less than 5g per day), eating a balanced diet rich in fruits and vegetables, regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight, avoiding tobacco use, and limiting alcohol consumption. Treatment involves lifestyle modification and antihypertensive medications such as ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, diuretics, and calcium channel blockers. Regular monitoring of blood pressure is essential for management. Source: World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines on hypertension.
