Basic first aid can save lives in emergencies. Key first aid procedures: For bleeding: apply direct pressure with a clean cloth, elevate the wound above heart level if possible, and call emergency services if bleeding is severe or does not stop. For burns: cool the burn under cool (not cold) running water for at least 10 minutes, cover with cling film or a clean plastic bag, do not apply ice, butter, or creams. For choking: for adults, perform abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver); for infants under 1 year, give 5 back blows followed by 5 chest thrusts. For CPR: push hard and fast in the center of the chest at 100-120 compressions per minute, to a depth of 2 inches (5-6 cm) for adults; give 30 compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths. For allergic reactions (anaphylaxis): use an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) in the outer thigh, call emergency services, and lay the person flat with legs raised if they feel faint. For seizures: clear the area of harmful objects, time the seizure, place something soft under the head, do not restrain the person or put anything in their mouth. Call emergency services if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, it is the first seizure, or the person does not regain consciousness. Source: UK National Health Service (NHS) First Aid Guidelines.
