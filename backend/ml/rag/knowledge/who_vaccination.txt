Vaccination is one of the most effective public health interventions, preventing 2-3 million deaths annually worldwide according to WHO. Vaccines work by training the immune system to recognize and fight pathogens. Key vaccines recommended by WHO include: BCG for tuberculosis, Hepatitis B (at birth), Polio (OPV/IPV), DTP (Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis), Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib), Pneumococcal conjugate (PCV), Rotavirus, Measles-Mumps-Rubella (MMR), HPV (for girls aged 9-14), and seasonal influenza (annually for high-risk groups). The COVID-19 vaccination campaign is the largest in history, with over 13 billion doses administered globally. Herd immunity thresholds vary by disease: measles requires 95% coverage, polio 80-85%, and COVID-19 70-90% depending on variant. Common side effects are mild and include sore arm, low-grade fever, and fatigue. Serious adverse events are extremely rare. WHO emphasizes that vaccination is safe, effective, and essential for global health security. Source: World Health Organization (WHO) Immunization Guidelines.
