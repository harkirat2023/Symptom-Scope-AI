Mental health is an essential component of overall health. According to the CDC, more than 1 in 5 US adults live with a mental illness. Common conditions include anxiety disorders (affecting 19.1% of US adults), depression (affecting 8.4%), bipolar disorder (2.8%), and schizophrenia (<1%). Warning signs of mental health conditions include: persistent sadness or irritability, extreme mood changes, withdrawal from social activities, significant changes in eating or sleeping patterns, difficulty concentrating, intense worries or fears, and thoughts of self-harm. The CDC recommends the following for maintaining good mental health: regular physical activity (30 minutes daily), adequate sleep (7-9 hours for adults), healthy eating, stress management techniques, social connection, limiting alcohol and avoiding drugs, and seeking professional help when needed. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (US Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). Treatment options include psychotherapy (CBT, DBT), medication (antidepressants, anxiolytics, mood stabilizers), support groups, and lifestyle modifications. Source: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) Mental Health Guidelines.
