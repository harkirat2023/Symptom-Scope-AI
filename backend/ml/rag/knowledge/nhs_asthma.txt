Asthma is a common lung condition affecting 5.4 million people in the UK according to NHS data. It causes occasional breathing difficulties ranging from mild to life-threatening. Symptoms include wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing, which are often worse at night or early morning. Triggers include allergens (pollen, dust mites, pet fur), respiratory infections, exercise, cold air, smoke, and stress. The NHS recommends a stepwise approach to asthma management: Step 1: Inhaled short-acting beta2 agonist (SABA) as needed. Step 2: Add inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) for regular prevention. Step 3: Add long-acting beta2 agonist (LABA) with ICS. Step 4: Increase to high-dose ICS + LABA. Step 5: Add oral corticosteroids or biologic therapies. Every asthma patient should have a personal asthma action plan. Emergency signs include: rapid worsening of symptoms, no relief from inhaler, inability to speak in full sentences, and blue lips or fingers. Call emergency services immediately in these cases. Source: UK National Health Service (NHS) Asthma Guidelines.
